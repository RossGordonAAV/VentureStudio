## Break-even logic
- Works **only if** a premium of roughly the US sports gap (≈3x mainstream) holds in NL/UK. UK incumbents already sell odour lines at mainstream prices (Persil Odour Defy ≈29p) – the premium has to be earned on proven efficacy (CSO's question), not branding. **Believed.**
- Subscription pays **only if** gross margin per order × orders retained > CAC. Neither CAC nor retention is public. **Known gap.**
- **Unbundling doubles fixed costs:** two SKUs = two MOQs, two PCN/label sets, heavier parcels. The system price must cover two products' margins. **Believed.**

## Scale check (Known)
- Smol, the UK laundry subscription benchmark: FY25 revenue £44.7m (+2.6%), pre-tax loss ~£1.5m, >120 staff, after a $34m Series B ([The Grocer](https://www.thegrocer.co.uk/news/smol-invests-for-growth-at-expense-of-rising-losses/717807.article)). Growth slowed from +37% in FY24 (Known); still loss-making.
- Blueland spent >$1m/month on social ads before profitability ([Modern Retail](https://www.modernretail.co/operations/how-blueland-is-refining-its-wholesale-strategy-after-launching-in-whole-foods/)). Dropps' CEO: "the era of cheap and easy online customer acquisition has ended"; ~80% of its ~300k loyal base prefer retail ([Packworld, Jan 2025](https://www.packworld.com/trends/ecommerce-d2c-packaging/article/22930028/dropps-detergents-journey-from-shelf-to-d2conly-to-omnichannel)).
- **Verdict as a scalable company:** only if efficacy is provably better *and* a cheap acquisition channel exists (gym/club partnerships, creators). Pure paid-social DTC needs VC-scale money. **Believed.**
