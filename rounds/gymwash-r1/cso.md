# CSO – Gym-wear laundry – R1 (21 Nov 2026)

**Claim the idea rests on:** residual odour in synthetic kit is a cleaning failure that existing chemistry (enzymes + activated oxygen + right temperature/sequence) can fix, and today's capsules don't deliver it.

## Why synthetics keep smelling – evidence grade: strong (peer-reviewed, replicated across groups)
- **Polyester binds sebum and bacteria.** Hydrophobic polyester adhered more bacteria and absorbed more sebum than cotton; bacteria form biofilms that survive washing, so the odour returns (Møllebjerg et al. 2021, Aarhus + Novozymes, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1128/Spectrum.01185-21)). **Known.**
- **Different microbes.** After a spinning session, polyester shirts smelled more intense and less pleasant; *Micrococcus* enriched almost only on polyester, staphylococci on both (Callewaert et al. 2014, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1128/AEM.01422-14)). Staphylococci make isovaleric acid from sweat leucine (BASF, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1002/mbo3.1174)). **Known.**
- **Washing can make it worse.** On synthetic T-shirts, washing cut odour volatiles but *increased* the load of intact bacteria; skin microbes were replaced by washing-machine microbes carrying biofilm genes; slow humid drying favoured Gram-negative odour formers (Díez López et al. 2025, Ghent + Givaudan, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12915-025-02147-5)). *Moraxella osloensis* produces the "damp laundry" compound 4-methyl-3-hexenoic acid ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1128/AEM.07816-11)) and is most abundant in door seals ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00253-026-13972-1)). Machines exchange bacteria between loads ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.3389/fmicb.2015.01381)). **Known.**
- **Cold, bleach-free washing is the driver.** Temperatures of >40–60 °C are needed for microbial control; below 20 °C enzymes and bleach become "essential" (Abney et al. 2021 review, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1128/AEM.03002-20); Bockmühl et al. 2019, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.15698/mic2019.07.682)). **Known.**
- Fabric softener and overdosing as causes: no peer-reviewed source found in this round – **Believed** (cationic/oily residue on a hydrophobic fibre), not proven.

## What works (existing technology)
- **Lipase + protease** (sebum, skin protein), **PDE enzyme** against "body grime" – Novonesis Pristine, launched 2021 ([release](https://www.inderes.dk/en/releases/new-enzyme-expands-the-role-of-biological-ingredients-for-laundry-detergents)); **DNase** against biofilm in Novozymes patents ([EP3551740B1](https://patents.google.com/patent/EP3551740B1/de)). Supplier claims; no independent sportswear trial found – **Known** they exist, efficacy on polyester **Believed**.
- **Percarbonate + TAED** (peracetic acid at 30–40 °C): standard antimicrobial bleach in powders. **Known** (review above).
- **Machine hygiene** (hot maintenance wash, dry door seal) and **fast drying**. **Known** (studies above).
- Antimicrobial textile finishes exist but disrupt the skin microbiome and wash out – out of scope for a detergent brand ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.3390/microorganisms9061192)).

## Ross's hypothesis: bleach kills the enzymes – partly right, mostly solved
- Peroxide does oxidise wild-type subtilisin (Met222); oxidation-resistant variants were engineered in 1985 (Estell et al., *J Biol Chem* 260:6518) and are standard. Powders have combined enzymes + percarbonate + TAED for decades. **Known.** So in-drum "cancelling out" is unlikely to be the main cause. **Believed.**
- Sequencing (enzymes first, bleach later) is already used: delayed/pH-controlled bleach release is patented ([US6225276B1](https://patents.google.com/patent/US6225276B1/enExample)). Two separate doses at home would at best copy this. **Believed.**
- **The real capsule compromise:** liquid capsules contain little water and **no percarbonate/TAED** (peroxide is unstable in liquids and with enzymes on storage) – so a capsule-only cold wash has no bleach at all. **Believed** (formulation logic; multi-compartment patents exist, [EP2014756B1](https://patents.google.com/patent/EP2014756B1/pl)). Ross's Vanish + capsule may be failing on temperature, biofilm and the machine, not on enzyme kill.

## Horizon (2–3 years)
1. **Cold-wash and anti-grime enzymes** keep improving (Novonesis Pristine line) – **headwind**: the big brands get the same ingredients first.
2. **New EU Detergents Regulation** published March 2026, applies from 23 Sep 2029; digital labels, refill labelling, microbial cleaning products in scope ([UL](https://www.ul.com/news/eu-publishes-new-detergent-regulation)) – **neutral**, mostly outside the 3-year window; probiotic "microbial" claims will need full strain disclosure.
3. Ghent/Givaudan and Henkel/Furtwangen work on wash-machine and humid-drying malodour – **tailwind** for a credible "kit + machine" system claim.

## Science test (quicker than the commercial test)
Ross's own kit: 3 washes each of (a) capsule at 30 °C, (b) oxygen-bleach powder with TAED (e.g. a "bio" powder) at 40 °C + hot empty maintenance wash first, (c) (b) + 30 min enzyme pre-soak; sniff after 24 h damp storage. If (b) fixes it, the gap is education and format, not chemistry.
