# CTO – Gym-wear laundry – R1 (21 Nov 2026)

**Core technical risk:** an unbundled "enzyme first, oxygen bleach second" routine must remove the smell in an ordinary home machine – and one of the two ways of sequencing (the drawer) may not work at all.

**What the parts tell us**
- Enzyme–bleach clash is real: peroxy-acid bleaches can deactivate enzymes "substantially instantly"; in one test activity fell from 84,000 to ~9.5 units (**Known** – [US4421664](https://patentimages.storage.googleapis.com/09/b9/c4/cc58c18031eb48/US4421664.pdf)). TAED + percarbonate makes exactly such a peracid, and is what lets oxygen bleach work below 60 °C (**Known** – [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bleach_activator)). Mainstream formulators already manage this clash in all-in-one products (**Believed**), so Ross's "activator kills the capsule" theory is plausible but unproven.
- Off-the-shelf odour enzyme exists: Novonesis Pristine (a PDE enzyme) breaks down "body grime" that traps odour (**Known** – [Novonesis](https://www1.novonesis.com/en/biosolutions/household-care/laundry/freshness/odor-control), [release](https://www.inderes.dk/en/releases/new-enzyme-expands-the-role-of-biological-ingredients-for-laundry-detergents)). Stock protease/lipase/amylase and Medley blends are sold via Univar (**Known** – [Univar](https://www.univarsolutions.co.uk/laundry-softners-emea/)); small-quantity terms not found.
- Sequencing in one cycle: put bleach in the main-wash compartment, softener compartment is for softener only (**Known** – [Samsung UK](https://www.samsung.com/uk/support/home-appliances/how-do-i-use-the-detergent-drawer-compartments-in-my-washing-machine/)). A cold final-rinse bleach step would work poorly (**Believed**). Workable sequence: **enzyme detergent in the prewash compartment, percarbonate/TAED in main wash** (**Believed** – needs testing per machine). Two-chamber pods exist ([Polyva](https://www.polyva-pvafilm.com/laundry-water-soluble-pods-high-quality-fragrance-double-chamber-detergent-capsules-laundry-pod-6778.html)) but both chambers release together – they separate storage, not timing (**Believed**).

**Regulatory basics (Known unless marked)** – Whoever puts their brand on it is the responsible party: name, address, phone on label; enzymes, perfumes, preservatives listed at any level; ingredient data sheet for medics; ingredient list on a website; load dosing for soft/medium/hard water ([HSE](https://www.hse.gov.uk/detergents/detergents-guidance.htm), [business.gov.nl](https://business.gov.nl/regulations/washing-cleaning-products/)). Hazardous mixtures need CLP labels and poison-centre notification with a UFI code ([EC](https://single-market-economy.ec.europa.eu/sectors/chemicals/poison-centres_en)); a percarbonate booster will likely be classified, so it needs CLP, UFI and an SDS (**Believed**). New EU Regulation 2026/405 applies from 23 Sep 2029: digital product passport, non-EU makers need an EU representative ([UL](https://www.ul.com/news/eu-publishes-new-detergent-regulation)). The contract maker normally supplies dossier/SDS (**Believed** – confirm).

**Fake-it-first (next test, two weeks, about 1 h/week):** Ross washes the same smelly kit 3 ways, 4 washes each, sniff-scored blind by a second person: (1) current routine (control); (2) **prewash compartment: bio liquid; main wash: plain sodium percarbonate + TAED booster, 40 °C**; (3) two separate cycles – bio wash, then oxygen-booster wash. If (2) or (3) clearly beat (1), the unbundled claim is worth building; if not, park the efficacy angle. Products used: off-the-shelf only [Ross: confirm which brands are to hand].

**IP:** stock enzymes and percarbonate/TAED are free to buy; the routine is not protectable – the moat is brand and instructions only (**Believed**).
