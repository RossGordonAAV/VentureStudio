# CEO brief – Gym-wear laundry brand – R1 (21 Nov 2026)

**One-liner:** A laundry brand for synthetic gym and sports clothing (polyester/nylon) that actually removes residual smell – possibly by unbundling the all-in-one capsule into separate, properly sequenced products (e.g. an enzyme step and an oxygen-bleach/odour step that don't cancel each other out) – branded for gym culture (Gymshark-style audience).

**Ross's own problem:** his synthetic gym kit comes out of the wash with a residual smell. A Vanish-type "activator" pre-wash plus his all-in-one capsule doesn't fix it; he suspects the pre-wash destroys the capsule's enzymes so they work against each other. Enzymatic pre-washes he tried also weren't effective. He sees an analogy with Ariel and others bundling everything into one capsule – maybe there's room to unbundle again.

**Studio rules that apply:** integrate existing technology (existing detergent chemistry, contract manufacturers/white label – no R&D programme); recurring revenue preferred (detergent is repeat purchase – subscription possible); a low-effort side business is also a valid outcome; showstopper first; straight to industry/customers; testability verdict required.

**Question for this round:** Why does synthetic gym kit keep smelling (the science), do existing sports detergents (and laundry practices) already solve it, and is there a gap for a gym-culture brand – possibly an unbundled system – that people would pay for repeatedly?

**Riskiest assumption (showstopper):** that the smell isn't already solved by existing sports detergents or simple practice changes (temperature, dose, vinegar, drying), and that a brand can differentiate on efficacy rather than just marketing.
