## Break-even logic (words, not P&L)
- Only works if a runner pays a premium for something incumbents don't do: **more than 30 ml in a short that doesn't chafe at 20 km+**. If Modibodi/Puma already pass the long-run test, this is a me-too product against brands with distribution – no.
- Bowel containment in a thin short is **not existing technology** (Believed). Treat it as out of scope; a bladder-only product must stand on its own.
- Recurring angle: a short washed after every run hits 50 washes in months, not years (Believed – depends on runs/week and pairs rotated, to find out). If true, a replacement subscription lifts the 1.8 orders/customer benchmark.
- With CAC of $30–70 against a ~£25–33 garment, one-off buyers barely pay back; margin needs either repeat or a cheap channel (running clubs, pelvic physios, race expos).
- Incontinence claims may bring the product under EU MDR (Class I, self-certified) – Believed; period-pant positioning may avoid it. To find out.

## Two ways to win
- **Scalable company:** only if the capacity/comfort gap is real and a replacement model holds. Crowded otherwise.
- **Side business:** plausible via stock style + logo (MOQ 50–300, samples $200–600, startup first order $8–15k per LJ Vogues). Set-up cost, hours/week and margin per unit: [Ross: confirm] after a supplier quote.

## Funding route (cheapest first)
1. Pre-orders / deposits from runners (also the demand test).
2. Own cash for a 50–300 pc stock-style run.
3. Angels only after repeat purchase is shown. Grants unlikely – no new technology.

## Red flags
1. Puma x Modibodi already sells a running-specific leak-proof short.
2. Absorbency ceiling of thin textiles vs a full bladder leak.
3. CAC vs single-garment ticket.
