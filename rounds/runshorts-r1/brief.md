# CEO brief – Leak-proof running shorts – R1 (20 Nov 2026)

**One-liner:** Running shorts or underwear for women in their 40s who have had children, that contain bladder (and possibly bowel) leaks during long runs, so they don't have to stop for the toilet.

**Who it's for:** Women in their 40s, post-childbirth, who run long distances. Source: Ross's wife and three friends – all in this group and all have the problem. Stress urinary incontinence when running is common after childbirth; "runner's trots" (exercise-associated bowel urgency) also exists.

**Studio rules that apply:** integrate existing technology only (absorbent/leak-proof textiles already exist – period pants, incontinence underwear); recurring revenue preferred, transactional fine if the market is big, a low-effort side business is also a valid outcome; showstopper first; go straight to customers/industry; testability verdict required.

**Question for this round:** Is there a real gap – are existing leak-proof underwear/activewear products (Thinx, Modibodi, Knix, TENA, Confitex, etc.) not good enough for long runs? If so, what exactly fails (capacity, chafing, odour, bowel leaks, look, washing), what would women pay, and what's the cheapest real-money test?

**Riskiest assumption (showstopper):** that existing products don't already solve this well enough – and that a thin running garment can credibly handle more than light bladder leaks.

**Evidence so far:** four women in Ross's circle. Nothing else checked.
