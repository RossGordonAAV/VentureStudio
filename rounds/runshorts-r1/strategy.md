## Why the gap exists
**WHY INCUMBENTS AREN'T DOING THIS**
- Activewear lines are built as period backup (Nike: with tampon) at 15–30 ml; incontinence capacity (60–100 ml) sits only in underwear (Thinx). **Known** [7][9]
- Both running-specific shorts from the market leader (Essity) are being cleared at 50% – suggests the category under-sold, not that it was never tried. **Believed** [1][4]
- Incontinence brands (TENA, Confitex) stay in "light leaks", older-women positioning; no sport product. **Known** [10][11]

**WHERE THE GAP APPEARS TO BE**
- A running short with 60–100 ml capacity, thin enough not to chafe over 15–30 km, sold in the EU. Only Lumana (US, ½ cup) claims it. **Believed**
- Bowel leaks: no product claims faecal protection; Saalt only suggests a "super gusset" for runner's diarrhoea. **Known** ([Saalt](https://saalt.com/blogs/news/milk-every-womens-marathon-running-with-leaks))

**RISKS TO THE GAP**
- Essity clearance may mean runners don't pay CA$90/AU$66 for shorts – they buy pads. **Believed**
- Capacity vs. thickness trade-off: "chunky"/"thicker" already flagged at 30 ml. **Known** [2][4]
- Leak volumes per run not found; 30 ml may already suffice for most. **Not found**
- Insert/physio route solves the cause; no review of shorts failing *on runs* found (capacity, odour, chafing complaints not found in visible reviews).

## Demand evidence (PubMed)
- 30.7% of female recreational marathoners report UI; 53% of those leak while running, half at end of race (n=517, mean age 41) ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.purol.2015.05.009)).
- Santiago marathon (n=366): SUI 33.9%, faecal incontinence 7.1%; age ≥35 and parity linked to SUI ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00192-026-06714-4)).
- Runners: anal incontinence symptoms 34.0%; vaginally parous runners 39.3% ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00192-020-04531-x)).

## Bowel-leak angle (separate)
Faecal leak in runners is real (7.1% [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00192-026-06714-4)) and unserved; a Dutch 2026 study found women cope by toilet mapping, spare clothes and dark kit, and avoid care out of shame ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1080/03630242.2026.2659040)). A thin short containing stool and odour is **Guessed** to need new materials work → treat as R&D; keep out of v1.

## Why we win / lose
- Win: capacity (60–100 ml) + running cut; EU absent; NL niche via run clubs. **Believed**
- Lose: Essity can lift capacity in one season; pads are cheap; category already clearing. **Believed**

**Why now:** leakproof textiles are commodity and pelvic-floor talk is mainstream (Nike, Saalt/MILK marathon). **Believed**

## Pre-mortem
1. No market need (pads/insert "good enough"). 2. Get outcompeted (Essity/Thinx). 3. Poor product (bulk/chafe at high capacity).

## Strategic test
Ask Ross's four runners to run 15 km+ in a Modibodi Running Short (30 ml) or Thinx 60–100 ml pants and report leak-through, chafing and odour – one week, cost [Ross: confirm]. If 30 ml holds, there is no gap.
