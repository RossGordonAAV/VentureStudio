# CEO synthesis – Leak-proof running shorts – R1 (20 Nov 2026)

## We believe
- **The problem is real and common.** 31% of women recreational marathoners (mean age 41) report bladder leaks, and 53% of those leak while running. Stress leaks are linked to age ≥35 and childbirth. 7% report faecal incontinence. (Known, PubMed)
- **Existing running shorts hold too little.** Modibodi's running short holds 15–30 ml; Knix's runs from "1 tampon" up to 30 ml. In a 38-minute treadmill test, runners with leaks gained a median 24 g on a pad, with a range of 4–166 g. Over a 2–3 hour run it's likely several times that. (Known; long-run volume Believed)
- **The category may have undersold.** Essity, owner of Modibodi and Knix, is clearing both running shorts at 50% off. Lumana (US only) is the only short claiming about ½ cup. Nobody serves the EU at incontinence capacity. (Known / Believed)
- **Bowel leaks can't be handled with existing textiles.** That would take R&D, which the studio rules out. Scope is bladder only. (Believed)
- **Building it is commodity.** Chinese OEMs make sport leak-proof shorts from a 50-piece minimum order, with samples in 72 h. The best route is a running short with a pocket for a swappable pad, so capacity rises without a thick short. (Known / Believed)
- **The real competitors stop the leak at source.** Uresta (a reusable insert, $139; 90% of runners still using it at 12 months) and Contiform pessaries; also pads, physio and planning routes past toilets. (Known)
- **The money:** transactional sales at £25–33 per short against $30–70 to acquire each customer. It needs repeat buys (a pair lasts about 50 washes; replacement pads are a possible subscription) or a cheap channel such as run clubs or pelvic-floor physios. (Known / Believed)
- **Regulation:** an "incontinence" claim may pull the product under the EU Medical Device Regulation. Incumbents stay apparel by talking about "bladder leaks". (Believed – adviser to confirm)

## We don't know
- How much these women actually leak on a 15–30 km run, and whether 30 ml plus a pad already works.
- Whether chafing is the bigger problem than capacity.
- Price and willingness to pay against Uresta or pads.

## Outcome type
- **Scalable company:** possible but contested. Essity can follow fast, and the category is being cleared. The repeat-pad model is the only recurring angle.
- **Side business:** plausible. A stock OEM short plus your own pad pocket, low minimum order, sold direct to NL running communities.

## Testability: Easy
The showstopper test needs four women, one long run each and a kitchen scale.

## Proposed decision: Keep going, showstopper first
No manufacturing or brand work until the existing products are shown to fail.

## Minimum next test (14 days)
- **Assumption:** existing leak-proof shorts fail on long runs (capacity or chafing).
- **Method:**
  - **Week 1:** your wife and three friends each do a ≥15 km run in a Modibodi or Knix short (plus a pad if they normally use one). They weigh garment and pad before and after, score chafing, leak-through and odour, then do a 20-minute interview each.
  - **Week 2:** only if week 1 shows failure, a pre-order page posted in 2–3 women's running groups. The hook is whatever failed.
- **Pass:** at least 2 of 4 have leak-through or bad chafing at ≥15 km, then ≥20 refundable deposits from strangers in week 2.
- **Kill:** the existing shorts work for 3 of 4, or fewer than 5 deposits from strangers.

## Red flags answered
- **CFO, acquisition cost vs price:** cheap community channel, plus the replacement-pad angle tested in interviews.
- **CTO, capacity per mm:** a pad-pocket design rather than a thicker core.
- **Strategy, Essity clearance:** test whether women still want it, not just whether it works.
- **Wedge, too close to incumbents:** narrowed to long-run capacity and chafing in the EU.
