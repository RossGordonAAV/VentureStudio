# CEO brief – Legacy refrigerant destruction – R1 (20 Nov 2026)

**One-liner:** Collect small (kilogram-scale) stocks of old CFC, HCFC and high-GWP HFC refrigerants from European holders and have them destroyed at a licensed plant, earning carbon credits per tonne CO2e destroyed.

**Who it's for:** Gas holders – old industrial plants and cold stores, installers and service firms with recovered gas, small holders (farms, SMEs, sheds and basements), car and lorry garages (old R-12 A/C). Paying side: carbon credit buyers.

**Ross's notes:** Scope CFCs (R-11, R-12), HCFCs (R-22), HFCs (R-404A, R-134a). Newer HFCs have lower GWP, so destroying them is worth less – the business case is a carbon credit case. Idea: take one or two canisters to the AEB waste-to-energy plant in Amsterdam to be burnt – unchecked whether AEB can or may destroy these gases. Ross knows companies already do this.

**Question for this round:** Is there money in sourcing kg-scale volumes in Europe and destroying them for carbon credits – and what's the cheapest real-world test to find out?

**Riskiest assumption:** That EU-sourced gas can earn credits at all (EU rules already require recovery and destruction, so additionality may fail), and that kg-scale volumes cover collection, transport, verification and destruction costs.

**Last round:** none (R1).
