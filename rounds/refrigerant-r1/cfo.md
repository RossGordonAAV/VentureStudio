**Bottom line:** The money works per kg for CFCs, and only if EU-sourced gas can earn credits. That is the number to find out first. HFCs at kg scale do not cover the chain.

- **Verification / registration fixed cost:** not found – **to find out**. Protocols deduct baseline/project emissions, so net credits per kg are below the gross figure – Believed.

## Break-even logic
Only if (a) the gas is credit-eligible, (b) the mix is CFC-heavy (R-12 gross ~$128–184/kg vs $18–48/kg chain cost), and (c) each batch is large enough to spread a fixed verification cost. R-22 and R-134a at kg scale do not clear the chain cost range – Believed. Minimum batch = fixed verification cost ÷ net margin per kg; neither figure is known yet.

## Eligibility (the riskiest assumption)
- **ACR International:** sources outside the US, **CFCs only**; reductions must exceed any legal requirement – Known [6].
- **EU law:** ODS in RAC equipment "shall… be recovered for destruction, recycling or reclamation" (Reg. 2024/590, Art. 20) – Known [7]. So EU-sourced CFC may fail the legal-requirement test – Believed.
- **CAR Article 5:** Article 5 (developing) countries only, destruction in the US – Known [8]; the EU is not eligible – Believed.
- **Verra VM0016 v2.0:** all Montreal Protocol Parties, HFCs newly in, additionality via VMD0048 – Known [9]. Whether EU CFC/HCFC passes is **to find out**.

## Red flags
1. EU additionality may fail outright, leaving a disposal-fee business, not a credit business.
2. AEB is not on the EU list of registered destruction facilities (June 2026; NL entries: Chemours, A-Gas, Eco-Collect, Westfalen) – Known [10]. Do not take canisters there.
3. Credit price is thin and opaque; two data points, $12.50–18/t.
