# CEO brief – Science Park grid batteries – R1 (20 Nov 2026)

**One-liner:** Buffer energy use with batteries (behind-the-meter peak shaving and/or a shared park battery) so that new buildings and tenants can be connected despite grid congestion – starting at Amsterdam Science Park, then scaling to congested industrial sites.

**Who it's for:** The Science Park as a whole – the park owner/developer and building operators. Ross works closely with Matrix Innovation Center (contacts Evo and Raymond), who are having issues building new facilities and often have to build semi-passive buildings because they can't get enough grid capacity. Ross notes congestion is a bigger issue on industrial sites than office/science parks, and many people are already looking at this.

**Ross's role:** would found a company.

**Question for this round:** Why isn't buffering already used to unlock capacity at the Science Park – technical, regulatory (Liander rules, group contracts / "groepscontract", non-firm ATO contracts, cable pooling), or economic? Are there quick wins, and is it scalable to industrial sites?

**Riskiest assumption:** That a battery actually lets a site get or use more capacity under Liander's/TenneT's rules (rather than just lowering peaks within an existing connection), and that someone will pay for it – and that the reason nobody has done it isn't fatal.

**Evidence so far:** a recent side comment from Evo (Matrix). Nothing checked.

**Last round:** none (R1).
