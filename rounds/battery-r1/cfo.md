# CFO – Science Park grid batteries – R1 (20 Nov 2026)

**Bottom line:** the money works **only if** the battery buys time or capacity a tenant cannot otherwise get. Tariff savings alone are small. Liander already sells the "battery for capacity" product (FlexPlus Batterij), so the question is less "is it allowed" and more "who pays the capex and what is a year of delay worth to Matrix".

## Break-even logic
- A behind-the-meter battery pays back only if (avoided delay or redesign cost) + FlexPlus/CSC fee + Flex-e 40% + residual trading > capex. Trading alone does not cover business batteries; they pay where the cost of *not* growing exceeds the investment ([CE Delft for Amsterdam, 2024](https://openresearch.amsterdam/image/2025/4/9/energyhubs_en_batterijopslag.pdf)). Known.
- Battery-as-a-Service (no upfront capex, share of savings) is an established model ([RSM, Jun 2026](https://www.rsm.global/netherlands/sites/default/files/media/Nieuwsitems/2026/RSM%20Report%20BESS%20Market%20Outlook%20june%202026.pdf)) – but needs a balance sheet Ross does not have. Believed.
- A shared park battery under a groeps-TO needs members on the same network ring; DSOs do not offer it as a standard product yet (CE Delft). Known.

## Funding route (cheapest first)
1. **Customer pays + Flex-e 40%** – Matrix (or the park owner) owns the asset; the venture sells design/ops. No capex on our books.
2. **Asset finance / BaaS partner** (energy fund or Rabobank-type lender) carries capex; the venture earns a fee or margin.
3. DEI+ for a novel shared-park set-up – only if genuinely a demonstration. Fit not checked.

## Red flags
1. Capex sits with someone – BaaS turns a start-up into an infrastructure fund.
2. CSC liability: parties feared open-ended liability for grid disruption; CSC 2.0 (Oct 2026) eased this, payment structure still under discussion ([Solar Magazine](https://solarmagazine.nl/nieuws-zonne-energie/i45037/capaciteitssturingscontract-2-0-is-klaar-niet-iedereen-durfde-te-tekenen)).
3. Liander already offers FlexPlus directly; the margin for an intermediary may be thin.
