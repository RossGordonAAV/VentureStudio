# Wedge check – Science Park grid batteries – R1 (20 Nov 2026)

**Wedge verdict: Wedge too close to incumbents** (park-level shared battery / energy hub). A sharper candidate exists (below).

**Why the Science Park wedge is weak**
- Park hubs are a crowded, maturing core. ~200 hub initiatives in NL, 6–7 operational; Kenter Groendus already sells hub EMS as a service (Known – [Solar Magazine, Apr 2026](https://solarmagazine.nl/nieuws-zonne-energie/i43470/collectief-energiemanagement-binnen-energiehubs-het-vraagt-om-vertrouwen-en-vergt-wat-moed)). Liander has signed its tenth group transport agreement (Known – [Solar Magazine, Oct 2026](https://solarmagazine.nl/nieuws-zonne-energie/i45058/liander-sluit-tiende-groepstransportovereenkomst-3-bedrijven-lelystad-gaan-transportcapaciteit-delen)). Grid operators must offer the GTO from 1 Jan 2027; Netbeheer NL says it is "no simple escape route" for waitlisted firms (Known – [Solar Magazine, Mar 2026](https://solarmagazine.nl/nieuws-zonne-energie/i43328/groepstransportovereenkomst-geen-simpele-ontsnappingsroute-voor-bedrijven-op-de-wachtlijst)). "Groepscontract set-up as a service" is therefore being commoditised and advisory-led (Believed).
- The anchor customer has already solved its own case. UvA's LabQ (700 kW, Science Park) went ahead without extra grid load. Haskoning measured actual FNWI peaks, cut ~600 kW through efficiency, and added only a *small* battery (Known – [Haskoning](https://www.haskoning.com/en/projects/uva-establishes-new-lab-through-clever-grid-congestion-strategy)).
- The binding constraint may be TenneT, not Liander. For 371 of 449 waitlisted Amsterdam applications, TenneT's grid is also limiting, so a flexible contract is not possible (Known – [Solar Magazine, Oct 2026](https://solarmagazine.nl/nieuws-zonne-energie/i44957/eindelijk-ruimte-op-het-amsterdamse-stroomnet-305-ondernemers-hebben-aansluiting-alsnog-binnen)). If Science Park sits in that group, buffering creates no new connection rights (Believed). Not checked for Science Park.

**Sharper wedge (candidate):** *new lab buildings on congested campuses whose requested connection is sized on design figures.* LabQ measurements showed actual lab demand well below design (Known – Haskoning, above). The offer would be: measure, right-size the request, then add a small battery to cover the gap. Matrix's semi-passive builds fit this pain (Believed; based only on the side comment from Evo).
- Risk: today it looks like engineering consultancy that Haskoning already delivers, so it is a feature, not a company (Believed). It becomes a company only if lab-load data feeds a repeatable sizing and control product (Guessed).

**Expansion path**
- Ring 2: other NL campuses. At Utrecht Science Park, UU expects diesel/gas generators and batteries, with the grid possibly full until 2031 (Known – [DUB](https://dub.uu.nl/en/news/uu-faces-extra-costs-due-power-supply-problems)). Leiden, Delft and Eindhoven not checked (Guessed).
- Ring 3: lab and cleanroom-heavy bedrijventerreinen, then EU campuses in congested grids (Guessed). Market size: not found.

**Question to answer next:** at Matrix/Science Park, is the bottleneck Liander MV or TenneT? And is the requested kW real load or design margin?
**Cheapest test:** one 30-minute call this week with Rowan Boeters (UvA), plus Evo/Raymond (Matrix), on the last refused connection request: MW asked, reason given, and whether the LabQ battery was decisive or marginal.
