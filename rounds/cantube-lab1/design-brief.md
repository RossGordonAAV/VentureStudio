# Design brief – Statiegeld can tube – Lab 1 (21 Nov 2026)

**User and setting:** Dutch household that collects empty drink cans (€0.15 statiegeld) in the kitchen, next to the bin or fridge, and takes them to a supermarket return machine (Tomra) every week or two.

**The one job:** store 10 uncrushed cans per tube in a small footprint, without drips or smell, and empty them quickly at the machine.

**Critical features (Known from Ross's home prototype):**
- Bore ~70.6–71 mm for a 66 mm can (air-cushion descent works; 2.5 mm radial gap).
- Lengths 1,170 mm (10 × 330 ml) or 1,520 mm (10 × 440 ml).
- Sealed, removable bottom (screw cap) so residue doesn't drip; unscrews at the machine.
- Must not tip: single 75 mm tube on its own is unstable → 3–4 tube cluster or wall bracket.
- Carryable to the shop (by bike or car).

**Already built:** 1.0 m grey Martens Ecomar 75 mm PVC with push-on end cap (holds 9 cans, tips). Planned V2 spec: 75 × 2.2 mm anthracite PVC-U pressure pipe, threaded screw-cap end piece, routed oak or printed base, printed top collar.

**Looks-like vs works-like:** works-like is solved. The open question is looks-like: from "drainpipe" to a homeware product someone pays a premium for over a €13–18 can bin.

**What each version must prove:**
- **V1:** a 3-tube cluster that stands, seals and looks decent enough to photograph for a Marktplaats listing – built within a week from shop parts.
- **V2:** a small batch (10–50) that looks like a homeware product (base, collar, carry handle, colour/finish), first units within a week, sellable at a premium price.
- **V3:** what a 1,000–10,000 unit product would look like and cost: stock extrusion vs custom, moulded base/cap, flat-pack box, shipping a 1.2 m parcel.

**Round 1 decision context:** keep going as a side-business test only; no tooling, no brand build. Shipping a 1.2–1.5 m parcel is the believed margin killer. Prices: `[Ross: confirm]` unless sourced.
