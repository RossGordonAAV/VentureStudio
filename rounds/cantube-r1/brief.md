# CEO brief – Statiegeld can tube – R1 (20 Nov 2026)

**One-liner:** A vertical tube that stores 10 uncrushed empty cans in a small footprint, seals drips with a screw cap, and empties straight into the supermarket statiegeld return machine; scales to a modular 3–4 tube home hub ("CanStack").

**Who it's for:** Dutch households returning cans since the €0.15 can deposit was introduced. Crushed cans are rejected by return machines, so households store bulky, sticky uncrushed cans. Channel ideas: Bol.com, Dille & Kamille, Blokker-type homeware stores.

**Ross's details:** see ross-summary.md in this folder (prototype in 75 mm PVC pipe; ~2.5 mm air gap gives a cushioned drop; V2 anthracite PVC-U 75 × 2.2 mm, 1,170 mm for 10 × 330 ml, 1,520 mm for 10 × 440 ml; screw-cap base in an oak socket; multi-tube hub; productised extruded tubes with injection-moulded/3D-printed modular bases).

**Ross's choices:** ambition = consumer product brand. Evidence so far = home prototype works; friends/family have asked for one. Showstopper Ross named = **will people pay?**

**Studio rules that apply:** integrate existing technology (no development programme); recurring revenue preferred, transactional only if the market is big enough – so size the Dutch (and other deposit-scheme EU countries') market and look for repeat/recurring angles; showstopper first; go straight to industry/customers for answers.

**Question for this round:** Will enough households pay enough for this to be a real consumer brand – what price, what volume, through which channel – and what's the cheapest test of willingness to pay this week?

**Riskiest assumption:** Households will pay for a dedicated can-storage product rather than use a bag, crate or a cheap existing bin.

**Ross (22:42):** a core question is also *is it testable?* – can willingness to pay be tested cheaply and quickly, with a real signal (money or pre-order)?
