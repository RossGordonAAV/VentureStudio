# CFO – Statiegeld can tube – R1 (20 Nov 2026)

**Revenue model: transactional** (one-off durable). No real recurring angle; add-ons (extra tubes, 250 ml sleeve, wall bracket) give repeat sales, not recurring revenue. A B2B variant (sports clubs, offices, canteens) is still transactional. So the market must be big – see below.

Big enough in principle – if price holds above the €13–18 bin anchor.

## Break-even logic
Works only if (1) households pay a design premium over a €13–18 bin that holds 20× the cans, (2) a 1.2–1.5 m tube can be shipped flat or short (two-part tube?) at a cost that leaves margin, and (3) tooling stays at 3D-printing level until volume is proven.

## Side business view (Ross's question)
- **Set-up cost:** pipe, fittings, saw, 3D printer/print service, oak, listing photos – [Ross: confirm]; no tooling needed at first.
- **Hours/week:** cut, deburr, bond, pack, ship, list, answer messages – [Ross: confirm per unit]; Believed ~manual per unit, so hours scale with sales.
- **Margin per unit:** price [Ross: confirm] – materials (pipe ~€6–11/tube from Ross's figures + caps/base to find out) – platform fee (above) – shipping (to find out).
- **Viable?** Believed: yes as a small Marktplaats/Etsy/Bol hobby **only if** oversized shipping is avoided (local pick-up on Marktplaats, or a split tube). Shipping is the swing cost.

## Funding route (cheapest first)
1. Pre-orders/deposits on Marktplaats or a one-page shop – customer money first.
2. Own pocket for a 10–20 unit 3D-printed batch.
3. Retailer PO (D&K/Blokker) funding a first run – only after sell-through data.

## Red flags
1. **Price anchor:** cheap high-capacity bins and free bags/crates (Riskiest assumption).
2. **Shipping:** 1.2–1.5 m parcel may eat the margin.
3. **Policy:** from 1 Jan 2028 more return points and a bigger role for **bulk machines** ([Rijksoverheid](https://www.rijksoverheid.nl/actueel/nieuws/2026/09/04/kabinet-stelt-makkelijker-statiegeldsysteem-voor)) – tipping a bag gets easier, weakening the "feed 10 cans fast" benefit.
