# Wedge check – Statiegeld can tube – R1 (20 Nov 2026)

**Wedge verdict: Dead-end niche as a consumer gadget; possible wedge only as a B2B2C return aid.** A PVC tube has no IP and no lock-in, so any homeware brand can copy it in a season (Believed). Price anchor already exists: a 95 L Songmics deposit bin holding ~200 cans sells for €12.89 on Amazon.de ([familie.de](https://www.familie.de/testberichte/mit-diesem-amazon-pfandbehaelter-bleibt-leergut-aus-dem-blick--01KKHZSP9WETN4TCYE53TRPCND)) – Known. Dutch advice pieces already point to cheap can racks on Amazon, lidded bins and bags ([ID.nl](https://id.nl/huis-en-entertainment/huishouden/schoonmaken/statiegeldblikjes-handig-en-hygienisch-bewaren-zo-doe-je-dat)) – Known. Transactional, low ticket, no repeat purchase: fails the studio's revenue rule unless the market is big. NL can volume: not found.

**The wedge (if any):** not households, but the party paying for the return gap. Verpact must hit 90% (cans 83% in 2025), invested >€220m in 2025 and has had fines threatened ([DutchNews](https://www.dutchnews.nl/2026/08/more-deposit-bottles-returned-but-still-short-of-legal-mandate/)) – Known. A branded tube/stack given out by Verpact or a supermarket as a "return more" aid is a sale to one buyer with budget and pain (Guessed).

**Expansion path**
- Ring 2: PET bottles (all plastic bottles ≤3 L get deposit from 1 Jan 2028, with bulk machines given a larger role – [Rijksoverheid](https://www.rijksoverheid.nl/actueel/nieuws/2026/09/04/kabinet-stelt-makkelijker-statiegeldsysteem-voor)). Bulk machines may weaken the "feed 10 in sequence" benefit – Believed.
- Ring 3: other deposit markets. Germany is mature and already crowded with cheap bins (Known, above). Belgium has no scheme yet; it may get a waiver if it hits 80% in 2026, otherwise the PPWR 90% target applies from 2029 ([Brussels Times](https://www.brusselstimes.com/2281826/new-eu-packaging-rules-will-belgium-introduce-deposit-on-bottles-and-cans)) – Known.
- Recurring angle (venues/offices): horeca has no take-back duty and already returns via wholesaler bags counted at telcentra ([KHN](https://www.khn.nl/nieuws/vrijwillig-statiegeld-blikjes-inzamelen-hoe-doe-je-dat)) – Known. A collection subscription would compete with an existing, free wholesaler loop (Believed). Not an open gap.

**Why the gap exists**
- WHY INCUMBENTS AREN'T DOING THIS: a low-value, copyable category, already served by €13 bins (Known); homeware brands haven't bothered with a NL-only niche product (Believed).
- WHERE THE GAP APPEARS TO BE: keeping cans undamaged. Machines reject crushed cans or cans with unreadable barcodes; Cleanupteam says 60–70% of litter cans are flattened ([Kassa](https://www.bnnvara.nl/kassa/artikelen/miljoenen-euros-aan-statiegeld-blijven-liggen-consumenten-leveren-blikjes-en-flesjes-niet-of-te-beschadigd-in)) – Known.
- RISKS TO THE GAP: Verpact is piloting acceptance of rejected/dented packaging (same source); bulk machines from 2028; instant copies.

**Next question and cheapest test:** Would Verpact or one supermarket chain buy/co-brand a return aid? One call this week beats a consumer WTP test, because a household win stays a gadget.
