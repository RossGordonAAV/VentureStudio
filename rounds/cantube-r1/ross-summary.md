## Project Summary: *Statiegeld* Can Dispenser

### 1. Proof of Concept & Core Physics

* **Initial Prototype:** Built using a standard 1.0 m length of 75 mm grey Martens Ecomar PVC pipe fitted with a red push-on end cap.


* **Pneumatic Cushion Effect:** The ~71.0 mm internal bore creates an exact 2.5 mm radial air gap around a standard 66 mm beverage can. Dropping an empty can traps a cushion of air underneath it, causing a controlled, vibrating descent rather than a hard drop to the bottom.


* **Key Limitations Identified:** The 1.0 m off-the-shelf pipe caps out at 9 cans instead of a clean 10-can batch, and a single tall column on a 75 mm footprint lacks freestanding stability.

### 2. Material & Design Iteration

* **Evaluating Bespoke Metals:** Custom-cut raw aluminium (76 × 2.5 mm, matching the 71.0 mm bore) paired with ribbed polyethylene insert plugs (*lamellenstoppen*) offers a clean, furniture-grade finish when seated in a routed oak plinth, though custom single-piece cutting and oversized shipping push costs to ~€45–€65 per tube.
* **Settled V2 Spec (Dark Anthracite PVC-U Pressure Pipe):** Switching to thick-walled **75 × 2.2 mm PVC-U drukbuis** (70.6 mm ID) delivers a denser, sleeker dark charcoal finish without the cost of custom metalwork (~€25–€32 for a 5 m stock length, yielding 3–4 full tubes).
* **Exact Cut Lengths:** **1,170 mm** for 10 × 330 ml cans, or **1,520 mm** for 10 × 440 ml cans, cut with a 24–32 TPI bi-metal hacksaw and deburred cleanly.
* **Removable Bottom Closure & Base:** Bonding a **75 mm threaded screw-cap fitting (*eindstuk met schroefdeksel*)** to the base prevents stale beer drips on the floor, allows the tube to lift out of a wooden socket or multi-tube cluster, and unscrews quickly to empty cans directly into the supermarket *statiegeld* machine.

## How This Might Grow

### 1. The Multi-Tube *Statiegeld* Hub (Home Utility)

Scaling from one tube to a **3- or 4-tube bundle** held together by a routed oak baseplate and a 3D-printed top collar solves stability permanently while turning a single prototype into a complete household sorting station:

* **30- to 40-Can Capacity:** Holds €4.50 to €6.00 worth of *statiegeld* in a compact ~16 × 16 cm footprint next to the kitchen bin.
* **Grab-and-Go Transport:** Each tube acts as its own rigid carrier. Instead of juggling noisy, sticky plastic bags in the boot of the car or a bike pannier, you pull two full tubes out of the base, drop them into the boot, unscrew the bottom caps at the supermarket machine, and feed 10 cans in rapid succession.
* **Dedicated Lanes:** Separate tubes for standard 330 ml cans, tall 440/500 ml cans, and slim 250 ml cans (using a 63 mm sleeve insert) keep batches sorted.

### 2. A Modular Consumer Product ("CanStack" / Dutch *Statiegeld* System)

Since the introduction of €0.15 *statiegeld* on cans in the Netherlands, almost every Dutch household struggles with storing sticky, uncrushed cans (since crushed cans are rejected by barcode scanners at return machines):

* **Why It Works Commercially:** Existing solutions are either bulky wire/plastic bins that take up massive floor space or open crates that smell of stale beer. A vertical, modular tube rack uses vertical dead space next to a fridge or bin, contains odours and drips via a sealed screw-cap drip cup, and keeps cans uncrushed.
* **Productised Execution:**
* **Tubes:** Extruded matte-black or brushed-anodised tubes in two modular heights (330 ml and 500 ml) with a magnetic or twist-lock drip cup at the base.
* **Modular Base & Clips:** Injection-moulded or 3D-printed interlocking bases (allowing customers to snap 1, 2, 3, or 4 tubes together) with a wall-mount bracket option and an integrated carry handle on each tube.
* **Retail Angle:** A natural fit for Dutch design/homeware retailers (like Dille & Kamille, Bol.com, or Blokker-style home stores) as a flat-packed or modular *statiegeld* organiser—potentially tested first via a small batch of 3D-printed base/collar kits paired with pre-cut tubes sold locally.
